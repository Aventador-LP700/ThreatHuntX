# 30 · QR-Code Phishing

Inspect a normalized URL verdict for a redirect discovered during QR inspection.

**Track** Beginner · 8 min · Identity

**Prerequisites** Read the baseline and compare event fields. No external tools are required.

## Investigate

**Attacker intent** QR-directed malicious URLs.

**Learning outcome** Review the QR origin, redirect verdict and message identifier. Correlate the recipient’s activity without using the URL.

1. Review the clean baseline. Mail gateway or attachment projection supplies the exercise evidence.
2. Consider the hypothesis. A QR-derived redirect classified as a credential lure supports a message and click investigation.
3. Replay the positive fixture and inspect THX-30. Review the QR origin, redirect verdict and message identifier. Correlate the recipient’s activity without using the URL.
4. Compare the noisy fixture. A legitimate event registration or facilities email can also contain a redirecting QR code.

## Contain and prevent

Quarantine related messages, check interactions and review sign-ins if recipients used the destination.

Use QR inspection and phishing-resistant authentication. Teach users to report unexpected QR sign-in prompts.

## Knowledge check

- Which evidence distinguishes the fixture from a normal QR message?
  - A redirect verdict indicating a credential lure
  - The presence of a QR code alone

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A redirect verdict indicating a credential lure. QR format is a delivery detail. Destination analysis gives the signal.

</details>

- Where could an analyst find follow-on impact?
  - Only the attachment filename
  - Identity and click records

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Identity and click records. A delivery verdict cannot establish whether a recipient interacted.

</details>
