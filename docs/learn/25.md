# 25 · Wiper-Like Activity

Review an inert audit summary for mass deletion of protected resources outside a maintenance scope.

**Track** Advanced · 15 min · Linux

**Prerequisites** Know the basics of Linux auditing and how to review authorization context.

## Investigate

**Attacker intent** Simulated destructive actions.

**Learning outcome** Review affected resource class, object count, writer and change record. Seek backup and storage audit confirmation.

1. Review the clean baseline. Linux audit projection supplies the exercise evidence.
2. Consider the hypothesis. A mass-delete observation affecting protected resources without approved maintenance needs immediate integrity triage.
3. Replay the positive fixture and inspect THX-25. Review affected resource class, object count, writer and change record. Seek backup and storage audit confirmation.
4. Compare the noisy fixture. Retention enforcement or approved disposal can affect large datasets.

## Rule and evidence

Count at least 1 observation(s) per `user` inside 300 seconds. Each distinct source record can count once.

| Field | Comparison | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `action` | `eq` | `"delete_summary"` |
| `observables.target_class` | `eq` | `"protected"` |
| `observables.objects_affected` | `gte` | `100` |

Review the following event-level exclusions before changing the rule.

- `observables.change_context` eq `"approved_disposal"`

Tuning guidance. Verify lifecycle policies in advance and never suppress deletion on protected data solely because of volume.

## Contain and prevent

Preserve storage logs, pause confirmed unauthorized activity and validate backup integrity before restoration.

Protect backups from the same identity plane and require review for large destructive changes.

## Knowledge check

- What distinguishes the positive summary?
  - Protected targets and unverified context
  - The raw count alone

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Protected targets and unverified context. Cache lifecycle operations can be high volume without the same impact.

</details>

- Why verify backups first?
  - Backups are always present
  - Recovery depends on their integrity and isolation

<details>
<summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Recovery depends on their integrity and isolation. Shared access and untested backups can undermine restoration.

</details>
